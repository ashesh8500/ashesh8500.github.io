Fractal Cognition

A paper-trading research system that records why a decision happened — not just what it bought.

status active · paper trading focus decision infrastructure claim profitability not established

Fractal is the system I use to study allocation as a process, not a one-shot optimizer output. Markets generate noise; most “strategies” collapse under honest logging. Fractal’s job is to make the path from observation → hypothesis → risk gate → simulated fill inspectable after the fact.

What this is not Not a live brokerage product. Not a performance claim. Paper trading only. If someone asks whether it “beats the market,” the honest answer from published context is: that has not been established.

What you can peer into

At a high level the loop looks like this:

  • Perception — market and context observations enter a workspace.
  • Hypothesis — a thesis / lens proposes what might matter next.
  • Allocation — a controller turns that into intended weights or trades.
  • Risk — gates can reject, shrink, or hold before anything is simulated.
  • Journal — every path (executed, rejected, held) is written with provenance.

The interesting artifact is the decision journal: thesis version, rationale, confidence, price context, and links between intent and fill reconciliation. That turns a trading experiment into something you can audit weeks later instead of a vague “the model said so.”

Why build it this way

I care about systems that stay honest under their own logging. If the journal can’t explain a trade, the trade doesn’t count as research. That bias shows up elsewhere in my work too — evidence trails in AuditLayer, leakage controls in spatial evaluation, ablations in the systems thesis.

What stays out of public view

Implementation details, private prompts, live credentials, and unreleased performance runs stay off this site. What you’re reading is the shape of the system and the standard it holds itself to.
