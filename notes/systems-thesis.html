Systems Thesis

Portfolio allocation treated as layered system optimization — walk-forward, ablations, and the limits of deployment.

scope 60-arm study method walk-forward stance negative results included

This study asks what happens when screening, weighting, covariance choices, and turnover costs are treated as interacting layers rather than a single magic objective. The point is behavior under constraints — not a leaderboard chase.

What the study covers

  • Walk-forward evaluation across three portfolio universes.
  • Screening and weighting choices under explicit turnover penalties.
  • Ablations that remove or swap components to see what actually moved results.
  • Deployment limits — where a clean backtest stops being an honest production story.
Research philosophy Understanding system behavior under constraints beats maximizing a single return chart. If an arm looks strong only because turnover was free, or because leakage snuck in, it fails the study.

How to read the public materials

The long-form deck and supporting notes live under the system-optimization portfolio page. Start there for charts, arm structure, and the fuller writeup. This note is the orientation layer: what question was asked, and what kind of answer counts.
